### Introduction

🚀 An intense weekend at the 𝐋𝐞𝐠𝐚𝐥 𝐇𝐚𝐜𝐤𝐚𝐭𝐡𝐨𝐧 𝐌𝐮𝐧𝐢𝐜𝐡 2026

Together with my team Eversores Burocratiae — the destroyers of analog bureaucracy —, we tackled ELTEMATE (Hogan Lovells)'s challenge: building an AI-Supported EU Data Breach Response Assistant.

Our solution focused on communication:
(i) Cross-domain translation (legal ↔ tech) to avoid misunderstandings;
(ii) AI-based expert-grounded validation guards to catch compliance risks, technical errors, missing information, and inconsistencies before they surface.

But beyond the solution itself, the real value came from the Hackaton experience:
💡 In a 3-minute pitch, clarity beats complexity.
🎤 A compelling story often outweighs a fully working PoC.
🧠 Product thinking > pure technical execution in hackathon settings.
🏗️ Proven enterprise patterns (workflows, approvals, dashboards) consistently win over “clever” innovative solutions.

One key takeaway for the pitch on stage: focus on what your solution enables — not on what you built.

It was also interesting to see how modern tools like AI-powered prototyping (e.g. Lovable and MagicalPatterns) are reshaping how quickly ideas can be communicated and validated.

Big thanks to the organizers (Legal Tech Colab, Liquid Legal Institute e.V., START Munich and Technical University of Munich) and challenge sponsors (ELTEMATE, Siemens, EQS Group and BMW Group) for creating such a high-energy environment. Already looking forward to the next one!

### Learnings

1. Show what you through >>> Show what you did
2. Beautiful Presentation on Stage >> Working PoC
3. Product Oriented Team >>> Technical Oriented Team
4. Innovative Solution >>> Robust, Proven Solution
5. Common Patterns Repeat (Workflow, Approval System, Versioning, Dashboards)
6. Mentors

1 and 2: The pitch must be presented in 3 minutes. That's the one single time the juri is going to evaluate the project. The juri simply don't have time to have a detailed look into the project code and the project details, since they're evaluating 8-12 groups on the last hackaton day and the whole thing has to be done within a 1-hour deadline for announcing the winners. For this reason, on stage you should only show your idea the best way possible. Even a simulated workflow often scores higher than a deep but invisible idea (and 90% of your ideas will be invisible in a 3-min pitch!).
The juri doesn't care if your implemented a technical solution which worked, if the solution you show on stage _could_ technically work (that means, if it's technically doable). Despite of it, most of them wouldn't admit that.

3: A technical oriented team will think about the best technical way of delivering a working technical solution. On a company context, that's extremly good. In a Hackaton, that's bad invested time, since we don't really need a _working_ technical solution, just the _idea_ of how it could work. 90% of the winning teams showcased their product use on Lovable (AI product generation) and they _may_ have integrated them with a backend. And to be honest, that is probably how it should be done, since the product was implementable/doable from the beginning.

4: Justification should always ground what you show, but in the practice you simply don't have time to _explain_ the justification on stage. That means, you should really choose carefully the best and justificable solutions, but you won't have time to say _why_ you choose them.

5: All winning projects utilized repeating enterprise patterns such as (Workflow, Approval System, Versioning, Dashboards), instead of focusing on a single, innovative out-of-the-box solution. I guess, that using enterprise patterns makes it easier to them to think of how the solution could integrate to their work.

6: Also, Mentors say what they put value into and how you could present and idea, but in this singular experience it was more inspirational than practice, since they didn't have the context. The Mentors you get it's really like a dice throw – different mentors will also have different preferences on the same subject.
